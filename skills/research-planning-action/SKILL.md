# Research Planning and Action Records

Turn research direction into a revisable account of what the researcher is doing now, what has changed, and what they may do next. Keep intellectual commitments, work records, and completion claims distinct.

## Scope

Preserve the hierarchy:

`broad theme -> problem cluster -> specific research problem -> independent paper -> researcher work item`

Use only the levels needed for the current decision. A broad theme may support several papers; recording a work item is not evidence that a question, claim, method, or contribution has been accepted.

Modes include:

- `programme-map`: compare broad directions, problem clusters, and candidate papers;
- `paper-portfolio`: distinguish papers, shared resources, dependencies, conflicts, and submission sequences;
- `current-stage`: record the work the researcher identifies as active, paused, blocked, or completed in the present stage;
- `next-stage`: record possible or confirmed work for the next stage, including dependencies and decision points;
- `state-update`: revise the project's existing canonical plan or action record after the researcher reports or designates a change;
- `reflect-and-replan`: at the researcher's request, examine what changed in understanding, evidence, method, limits, or direction, then revise the plan if authorized.

Combine modes only when they serve one connected request.

## Authority and Status

Start from the researcher's stated aims, designated artifacts, constraints, deadlines, and existing plan. Do not infer authority from recency, filenames, or file existence.

Keep status explicit where ambiguity matters:

- `candidate`: proposed but not chosen;
- `researcher-confirmed`: accepted as the current direction;
- `in progress`: identified by the researcher as underway;
- `blocked`: cannot proceed without a named input, decision, or external change;
- `completed`: reported or confirmed by the researcher as finished;
- `suspended` or `superseded`: retained for history but no longer current.

Do not convert an exploratory topic into a planned paper, a venue possibility into a target, or a model suggestion into project status.

## Planning

For each consequential work item, capture only what helps the researcher orient the work: purpose, current state, required input, intended output, dependencies, next decision point, and any real deadline. Prefer a short current-and-next record over a comprehensive inventory.

When comparing routes, make tradeoffs visible: intellectual importance, distinct contribution, evidence and method needs, maturity, uncertainty, dependencies, opportunity cost, venue or audience fit, time, collaboration, and retained future options. Use qualitative judgment rather than invented scores or acceptance probabilities.

Reuse the project's existing canonical plan, roadmap, or task record. Do not create a second tracker, dashboard, registry, or status file merely because planning occurred.

## Meaning of Action

In this skill, `action` means a record of the researcher's work: what they are working on now, what they have completed or paused, and what they intend to do next. It does not mean an action to be performed by the agent.

Do not perform, invoke, schedule, or coordinate the reading, analysis, writing, language editing, review, or publishing tasks listed in a plan. Those capabilities remain independently requested research work. This skill may record their status only from information or artifacts the researcher supplies or designates.

Update an existing plan or action record only when the researcher asks for that record to be created or revised. Do not mark a work item complete merely because a file exists, prose is polished, or the agent infers that sufficient work has occurred.

## Reflection and Replanning

Reflection is an on-demand planning mode, not an automatic ceremony after every stage. Begin from the researcher's question and the artifacts they place in scope. Distinguish changes in the researcher's understanding from source findings, empirical results, and model observations. Do not write first-person conclusions on the researcher's behalf.

Revise priorities or direction only when the researcher authorizes that change. A reflection may end with no plan change.

## Return

Lead with the current decision, plan, or current-and-next action record. Report assumptions, model-added structure, researcher-reported progress, blockers, and decisions still owned by the researcher. Treat all programme maps, priorities, status interpretations, and replanning judgments as researcher-reviewable.

Never fabricate progress, deadlines, dependencies, venue facts, collaborator commitments, or completion evidence.

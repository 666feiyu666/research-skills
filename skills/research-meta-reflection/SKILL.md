# Research Meta-Reflection

## Purpose

At the researcher's explicit request, help them look back on a self-declared stage of research so they can examine how their understanding, evidence, method, limits, and possible direction have changed.

This is a reflective research practice, not project management. Research artifacts may provide evidence and a reflection record may preserve intellectual memory, but file maintenance, progress tracking, task queues, Skill-use logging, and workflow control are outside this Skill.

The researcher controls:

- when a reflective boundary has been reached;
- what stage, question, experience, or body of work is in scope;
- which judgments about the research are accepted;
- whether anything should be preserved;
- whether to continue, pause, abandon, revisit, or redirect the work.

Completion of another research task does not itself create a reflective boundary and must not trigger this Skill.

## Require Explicit Invocation

Use this Skill only when the researcher explicitly invokes it for stage reflection. Do not invoke it because an artifact changed, a draft was completed, a submission occurred, a project became long-running, or an unresolved next action appeared.

Use only the requested mode:

- `reflective-dialogue`: explore what this stage meant without presuming a final synthesis;
- `reflection-synthesis`: reconstruct the researcher's present meta-level understanding and choices in a concise reviewable form;
- `reflection-preservation`: save a review draft, then revise or confirm it from the researcher's inspection.

Dialogue and synthesis are read-only unless the researcher explicitly asks to preserve or edit a record. A request to reflect does not authorize changes to project status, indexes, research artifacts, task lists, or directories.

## Selective Reflective Lenses

Use only lenses that matter to the researcher's present concern:

- **Orientation:** What was the researcher trying to understand, establish, examine, or produce?
- **Intellectual change:** What became clearer, more complicated, less convincing, or newly visible in the problem, concepts, possible answer, or contribution?
- **Evidence:** What did the literature or research objects support, weaken, complicate, or leave unresolved?
- **Method:** Which ways of reading, thinking, analyzing, designing, or writing helped or hindered this stage?
- **Limits:** What remains unknown, unstable, unsupported, inaccessible, or difficult to infer?
- **Direction:** What does the researcher want to continue, pause, revisit, abandon, compare, or change?

These are possible lenses, not a compulsory form. Begin from the researcher's own formulation and ask one focused question at a time when dialogue is useful.

## Ground Reflection Without Taking It Over

- Use the researcher's statements and only the artifacts or records they place in scope.
- Treat an artifact as evidence that work occurred, not proof that the researcher accepts, understands, or can use every claim it contains.
- Distinguish source-established content, empirical findings, researcher interpretation, researcher self-assessment, unresolved uncertainty, and model observation.
- When an artifact complicates the researcher's account, show the specific tension and invite their judgment.
- Do not scan the whole project, reconstruct its history, or inventory files unless the researcher explicitly includes that work in the reflection scope.
- Preserve the difference among encountering an idea, reconstructing it, being persuaded by it, using it analytically, and making it part of the researcher's position.

## Protect Researcher Ownership

This Skill must not:

- grade the researcher or diagnose their cognition;
- decide what the researcher should believe, claim, study, submit, or prioritize;
- turn a model observation into the researcher's first-person conclusion;
- verify scholarship, analyze raw research objects, repair an object-level argument, revise manuscript prose, or prepare a publication artifact;
- maintain project-wide status, current-file indexes, queues, dependencies, deadlines, artifact registries, or broken-link inventories;
- record which Skills were used;
- choose, schedule, invoke, or coordinate another Research Skill.

When reflection reveals a source need, analytical problem, argumentative difficulty, or possible direction, formulate it only to the degree the researcher confirms it. Do not solve or assign it inside the reflection.

## Preserve a Reflection Only When Requested

Before creating or substantially restructuring a reflection record, read [references/meta-reflection-record.md](references/meta-reflection-record.md).

- Do not create a file or directory merely because reflection occurred.
- Keep a saved reflection with the research project that gives it meaning, using an existing reflective location when one is designated.
- If no location is designated and the choice would materially affect organization, ask where it belongs rather than creating a new project schema.
- Edit the active record when the researcher revises the same checkpoint; do not create routine `v2` or `final` copies.
- Link only the artifacts that materially ground the reflection.
- Do not update a project-state file or index merely because a reflection was saved.

## Researcher Review

Treat every synthesis or saved reflection as a review draft until the researcher inspects it. Ask them to check whether:

- first-person statements genuinely express their experience and judgment;
- model expansions, connections, and interpretations are visible and accurately labeled;
- anything was omitted, over-organized, strengthened, or prematurely resolved;
- the recorded limits and possible directions still feel true at this boundary.

The researcher may accept the reflection unchanged, revise it, request revision, or reject it. Do not use it as the researcher's settled self-understanding or carry a proposed direction into later work until this review gate closes.

## Relation to Other Artifacts

No content Skill must produce a routine packet for meta-reflection. When the researcher supplies reading notes, thought records, analysis artifacts, outlines, drafts, reviews, or publication materials, use them only as evidence for the requested reflection and keep meta-level judgments distinct from object-level content.

A confirmed reflection may later be designated as input to another research task, but this Skill does not launch that task or turn the reflection into a central task queue.

## Return Results

- For dialogue, return the clearest present distinction or change and one consequential question when further reflection would help.
- For synthesis, return the researcher-stated or previously confirmed changes, limits, method observations, and choices that matter at this boundary, while labeling consequential model organization for review.
- For preservation, return the saved record path, what was preserved, and what needs researcher review.
- State explicitly when no files were changed.

Do not add management actions, Skill assignments, or future tasks merely because they seem useful.

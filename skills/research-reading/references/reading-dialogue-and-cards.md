# Source Records, Reading Sessions, and Semantic Cards

Use this reference when creating a source record, conducting or preserving a multi-turn source-grounded dialogue, or creating or revising a knowledge or question card.

## Three artifacts, three functions

These artifacts are related roles but do not form a required chain and do not contain one another:

| Artifact | Function | Typical trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source record | Provide a compact work-level index of identity, actual discussion coverage, reading sessions, and cards | “Register this source”; “create or update the source page” |
| Reading session | Preserve the actual chronological reading dialogue and how understanding changed | “Save/pause/close/synthesize this reading”; “make a reading note” after substantive dialogue |
| Semantic card | Preserve one researcher-selected essence of the discussion: a stable relation or an open question | “Make a card from this distinction/question”; researcher accepts a card candidate |

The source record owns source identity and access, actual reading coverage, and the register of sessions, knowledge cards, and question cards associated with the work. It may give each linked artifact a short retrieval description and review state. It must not reconstruct the whole argument, reproduce the dialogue, duplicate card content, or include proposal relevance and model-proposed extensions.

The session owns chronology and provenance. Preserve the researcher's questions and the answers given at the time. Do not replace that path with a polished summary.

The card owns selective semantic retrieval. It distils one result or question chosen and shaped by the researcher. It does not preserve the whole conversation and cannot replace the source record or session.

Use separate files. A “researcher processing” section appended to a source record is still a role violation. If the researcher requests two or three functions, create two or three linked artifacts.

When “reading note” is ambiguous, route by context: after a substantive dialogue it usually means a reading session; a request to register or create a home page for a paper/book means a source record; a request for the essence or key question means a card. Ask one focused question if the intended role remains unclear. A requested durable author-summary is not automatically a source record; keep it in a requested session or create a distinct processed note when explicitly requested.

## Source records

Treat the source record as a work-level hub, not as a surrogate for the work. Preserve only metadata needed to identify and recover the source, the evidence state and portions actually engaged, and a linked register of the reading sessions and researcher-selected cards that arose from it. Give each link only enough description to support later retrieval.

Do not fill untouched chapters or arguments, and do not copy detailed claims, quotations, dialogue, or card prose into the source record. When the researcher connects the source to a project or proposes an extension, preserve that turn in the live dialogue or a requested durable artifact. Create a standalone processed note only when the researcher explicitly asks for that distinct thematic output.

## Storage namespaces and navigation maintenance

Follow the current project's declared storage convention. A folder is a retrieval namespace, not a new artifact role. One-off artifacts may remain flat; sustained reading may use author and work subfolders. Keep cross-source cards outside a single-work namespace unless the project declares another canonical home.

Creating, moving, or renaming an authorized durable reading artifact includes the following same-source maintenance:

1. ensure that the work-level source record exists when the project requires one;
2. update only verified identity, actual coverage, the artifact register, review state, and maintenance date;
3. keep direct links between the source record and the session or card valid in both directions;
4. repair links among moved artifacts and check that all affected relative paths resolve.

This maintenance does not authorize another session, card, processed note, synthesis, project application, interpretation, or downstream update. For a multi-source artifact, register it only in the source records for works actually used and preserve each work's evidence state. Do not wait for a book or paper to be declared complete: synchronize navigation whenever a durable artifact is written or relocated, then update final coverage only if the researcher later declares the reading complete.

## Dialogue before artifact

Let the researcher's actual questions determine the local reading sequence. Answer the immediate question, reconstruct the relevant passage or argumentative move, and distinguish:

- what the source clearly supports;
- what is a reasonable interpretation;
- what remains uncertain or disputed;
- what requires another passage or source.

Reflect the researcher's expressed understanding accurately before evaluating or extending it. When a correction matters, retain the supported part and identify the smallest departure: a changed concept, omitted qualification, expanded scope, or unsupported inference. If the available text cannot decide, preserve the uncertainty.

Do not turn the dialogue into a quiz, a whole-book lecture, or a routine sequence of objections. Ask at most one focused follow-up question when it materially clarifies the same inquiry.

## Checkpoints and session records

Treat the live dialogue as transient working state. A multi-turn exchange becomes eligible for preservation but is not saved until the researcher confirms a checkpoint by:

- asking to save, pause, close, synthesize, or change phase;
- accepting a brief checkpoint proposal.

Ask once when a coherent line has reached temporary stability, the topic is changing, several reusable units have emerged, or the discussion is becoming difficult to recover. Continue without saving if the researcher declines.

A session record is chronological provenance, not a source summary or argument map. Preserve substantive questions in order and keep researcher wording when practical. If an answer is shortened, mark the compression and do not strengthen it. Exclude checkpoint negotiation, card logistics, and routine tool details from the numbered dialogue.

When an earlier answer would mislead later reading, append a concise correction or link to the corrected artifact. Never silently repair the historical record. A later card does not replace the session.

## Card selection

Card creation is optional. At a checkpoint, propose candidates only when they would improve later retrieval; create a card directly when the researcher requests one. For each proposed candidate, give:

- proposed title;
- proposed type;
- one sentence explaining why the type fits.

The researcher may accept, reject, defer, rename, retype, merge, or split candidates. Saving a session does not authorize a card.

Use a `knowledge-card` when a source-grounded concept, distinction, relation, or argumentative movement is stable enough to explain independently. Use a `question-card` when a consequential ambiguity, disagreement, missing bridge, or evidence need remains open. Recommend no card when the exchange is still local, fragmentary, or not independently useful.

## Researcher-shaped semantic center

Before drafting a selected card, recover the researcher's existing contribution from the dialogue: the problem wording, proposed relation, distinction, uncertainty, example, or practical association. Do not ask for repetition when this already supplies the semantic center.

If the center is missing or materially ambiguous, ask one focused construction question, such as:

- knowledge card: “What relation do you most want this card to clarify?”
- question card: “What part of this question remains genuinely unresolved for you?”

The researcher owns the semantic center, conceptual priority, retained interpretation, personal significance, and acceptance of model-proposed extensions. The agent may reconstruct, source-check, expose overreach, add verified context and locators, offer alternatives, and improve structure and readability.

Integrate a confirmed center into the card rather than duplicating it in separate “researcher contribution” and “model refinement” sections. Keep a separate provenance label only when a consequential difference remains unresolved.

## Knowledge cards

A knowledge card should remain intelligible after the conversation has been forgotten. Its minimal movement is:

1. compactly state the relation or logic;
2. explain why the concepts have that relation;
3. preserve enough source context and limits to recover the basis;
4. retain an open edge only when it matters.

Do not turn a model inference into settled knowledge. If the proposed unit is not yet adequately supported or researcher-shaped, continue the dialogue or use a question card.

## Question cards

A question card preserves an open problem as a productive entrance. It should state:

- how the question arose;
- what is currently established;
- what remains uncertain or disputed;
- at least one concrete route back into inquiry: a passage, an additional source, or a specific question;
- relevant source and artifact relations.

When later work changes the question, update its status and link the resulting answer or knowledge card. Do not silently rewrite the original uncertainty into a claim.

## Provenance and reuse

Use localized equivalents of these markers only where ambiguity would matter:

- `[quotation]`
- `[source reconstruction]`
- `[secondary reconstruction]`
- `[model interpretation]`
- `[researcher thought]`
- `[needs verification]`

For a Zotero-located source, preserve stable item and attachment keys when useful; do not preserve the absolute storage path as durable identity. For partial sources and web reconstruction, state coverage limits that affect the card.

Cards may later be designated as input to research thinking or writing, but they do not automatically change a project position, outline, or draft. Return every created or materially revised session or card for researcher review.

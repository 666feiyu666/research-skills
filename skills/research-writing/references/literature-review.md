# Literature Review: Bibliographic Checks Only

Apply this reference to literature-review tables, outlines, prose, and passages performing the same literature-review function elsewhere in a manuscript. The researcher performs all interpretation and decisions about scholarly use. Researcher review of agent-generated intellectual content is not an alternative to this division of responsibility.

## Permitted checks

- Check citation numbers, citation keys, titles, author names, publication years, edition or translation identifiers, and bibliographic identifiers such as DOI or ISBN.
- Check that an in-text marker corresponds to the intended reference entry and that numbering or keys remain consistent. Report ambiguous matches instead of choosing a work from its apparent topical relevance.
- Identify the edition actually designated or supplied and report metadata discrepancies. Do not choose which edition the study should use, merge distinct editions, or infer intellectual differences between them.
- Verify metadata against an identifiable bibliographic record or the supplied edition's title and copyright pages. Record the basis of a correction; leave unresolved information unverified.

The presence of a citation can be checked mechanically. Whether the cited work supports the surrounding proposition is a substantive judgment reserved for the researcher. Do not supply a missing citation by deciding which work would support a claim.

## Researcher-owned content

Do not generate, complete, revise, or evaluate source summaries, conceptual interpretations, reconstructed arguments, scholarly contributions, limitations, disagreements, cross-source comparisons, thematic groupings, inclusion or exclusion rationales, research gaps, or proposed roles in the study. This applies even when the result would be marked provisional, agent-proposed, or awaiting confirmation. Do not use leading questions to supply an interpretation indirectly.

Reuse the designated artifact and its existing format. Substantive table fields are filled by the researcher; do not populate them from source notes, abstracts, full texts, or earlier model output. Do not reorganize rows by inferred intellectual relationships. No table or CSV is required for a bibliographic check.

On explicit instruction, transcribe researcher-supplied content verbatim or change its formatting while preserving wording, relationships, and order. Do not turn table entries into review prose, paraphrase or compress an existing review, or supply transitions that explain a source's role. Approval of a table or outline does not authorize these activities under this boundary.

## Procedure and completion

1. Identify the designated artifact and the bibliographic fields or citation markers to check. Read only what is needed for those checks.
2. Verify identity, edition metadata, and citation correspondence without assessing the literature's meaning or use. Apply authorized mechanical corrections in place, preserving substantive content.
3. Report corrections with their verification basis and exact unresolved locations. If resolution requires interpretation or a scholarly choice, leave it to the researcher without suggesting an answer.

Describe completion as bibliographic or citation-marker verification only. It does not certify that the literature has been understood, that a citation supports a claim, or that the work belongs in the review. Do not change source-reading coverage or intellectual review status because metadata has been checked.

General drafting, auditing, polishing, and conversion modes cannot bypass this boundary, nor can another Skill be invoked to perform the excluded work as part of this task. Independent reading dialogue remains outside this workflow's scope.
